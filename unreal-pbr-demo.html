Feel a PBR material

Unreal's Material Editor has dozens of inputs but only three really change how a surface looks. Learn these and you'll stop fighting the renderer.

custom M 0.00 · R 0.50 · RGB (200,40,40)
Metallic0.00
Roughness0.50
Base Color(200,40,40)

What each knob does

  • Metallic is binary in real life. 0 = dielectric (wood, plastic, skin). 1 = pure metal. In-between values mostly mean dirt or a layered material.
  • Roughness is the everyday workhorse. Crank it up for chalk and felt; drop it for mirrors and chrome.
  • Base Color on a metal is the reflection tint. Gold's yellow comes from its metalness, not a coat of paint.
  • Specular? Leave it alone for 99% of materials. It's a holdover that confuses more than it helps.
Show me the Unreal C++ 
UMaterialInstanceDynamic* DynMat = UMaterialInstanceDynamic::Create(BaseMaterial, this);
StaticMeshComp->SetMaterial(0, DynMat);

DynMat->SetScalarParameterValue(TEXT("Metallic"),  0.0f);
DynMat->SetScalarParameterValue(TEXT("Roughness"), 0.4f);
DynMat->SetVectorParameterValue(TEXT("BaseColor"),
    FLinearColor(0.85f, 0.15f, 0.12f)); // a juicy red plastic
